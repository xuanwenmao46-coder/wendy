Chapter 3 · The Three Walls

Friendship requires three things
adults rarely have.

Proximity

Repeated, unplanned contact is how friendships naturally form. Adult life trades open-ended time for scheduled obligations — killing the serendipity that sparks connection.

Openness

Vulnerability accelerates closeness. Adults develop emotional armour — a protective layer of professionalism and self-sufficiency that keeps others at arm's length.

Repetition

Familiarity builds trust. But adult schedules are siloed — work, gym, commute — with few recurring contexts where someone encounters the same new person again and again.

Chapter 6 · The Way Forward

Friendship in adulthood
is an act of will.

It doesn't happen by accident anymore. But the research is clear: intentional, consistent effort rebuilds what life eroded.

01

Create recurring contexts

Join a club, class, or team that meets weekly. Repetition does the work that school once did automatically.

02

Lower the bar for initiation

A short walk, a coffee, a text. The ask doesn't have to be grand — it just has to happen. Rejection is rare and temporary.

03

Be the one who shows up

Consistency signals care. One reliable presence creates the safety that turns acquaintance into friend.

Connection is
still possible.
It just takes
courage.

The biology never changed. Only the infrastructure did.
Rebuild it — one honest conversation at a time.
