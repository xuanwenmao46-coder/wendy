# Motion Graphic Audit

Run before rendering any clip that should feel dynamic.

## Energy

- Does the clip have at least one clear hit moment?
- Are there 3-6 distinct beats instead of one slow reveal?
- Is there a contrast between fast motion and readable holds?
- Does the ending lock into a strong final frame?

## Focus

- Does motion direct the eye to the most important text/object?
- Are secondary elements quieter or delayed?
- Is there one hero motion, not five competing hero motions?
- Do flashes and bursts support the message?

## Typography

- Are text blocks split intentionally: block, word, or letter?
- Does important text land still for at least 0.5s?
- Are dense sentences avoided during high-energy motion?
- For Chinese text, are phrases animated more than random individual characters?

## Timing

- Micro entrances are 0.2-0.4s.
- Hero hits are 0.6-1.2s.
- Stingers are 0.4-1.0s.
- Details do not enter all at once.
- Long travel uses anticipation, overshoot, or smear illusion.

## Safety And Legibility

- No high-contrast flashing over 3Hz.
- No full-screen rapid flicker that could cause discomfort.
- Vital information stays inside safe margins.
- The clip is understandable even without audio.

## Anti-Patterns

Reject or revise:

- Every element fades up with the same easing.
- Motion is slow, polite, and identical across the whole clip.
- Text is always static while only cards move.
- Too many tiny readable elements flash too quickly.
- Random particle/glow decoration without a hit point.
- Motion makes the message less readable.
- The clip feels like a PPT slide with a transition preset.

## Acceptance Bar

The clip should feel like:

> A designed motion-graphics sequence with readable impact.

Not:

> A static slide where things gently appear.
